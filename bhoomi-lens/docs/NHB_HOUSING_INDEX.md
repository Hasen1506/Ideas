# National Housing Bank housing-index context

The app includes NHB's **HPI @ Assessment Prices**, a city-wide index constructed from lenders' valuations of residential properties. It is a housing-market indicator, not an observed vacant-land price, a plot valuation, a transaction panel, or evidence of an infrastructure project's causal impact. The [NHB methodology](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/Dashboard/About) describes changing methods, coverage and index bases. NHB's [copyright policy](https://www.nhb.org.in/copyright-policy/) permits accurate reproduction with prominent acknowledgment, excluding third-party copyrighted material. The app attributes the data to NHB and links each publication.

The archived series contain 50 named cities in each of two separate publications:

- FY 2012–13 base: 16 quarterly observations from 2013 Q2 to 2017 Q1, from NHB's [2017 publication](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/DataUploadFormats/QuarterlyBooklet_English/April13-June-13-To-January17-March-17.pdf).
- FY 2017–18 base: 30 quarterly observations from 2018 Q2 to 2025 Q3. The [September 2022 booklet](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/DataUploadFormats/QuarterlyBooklet_English/English%20Quaterly%20Booklet%20Jul-Sep%202022.pdf) supplies the first 18; three later NHB annual reports supply the remaining observations. The series uses a four-quarter weighted moving average. Publications round most observations to whole index points; the 2022 booklet reports its last quarter to two decimals.

The first series does not include the intervening quarters through March 2018, and the second archived snapshot ends in September 2025. Those are gaps in this app's **selected extracts**, not a claim that NHB published no other periods. City names are preserved from each publication; a few spacing variants are matched for display. Chandigarh's earlier sources say Chandigarh (Tricity), whereas the 2022 source says Chandigarh, so boundaries should not be assumed identical.

The three later annual-report tables are transcribed in `data/nhb_hpi_transcribed.json` from the official NHB PDFs as indexed by a search service on 13 November 2026. The original PDF bytes were not directly available in this workspace, so those three tables have source links but no PDF hash. Every table contains 50 cities; the overlap between successive publications agrees for all 50 after accounting for rounding in the September 2022 booklet. Those checks do not independently verify every transcribed value. The earlier PDFs were downloaded directly and their SHA-256 hashes are retained. `scripts/collect_nhb_hpi_archive.py` rebuilds the snapshot using the two PDFs and the checked transcription; it rejects changed headings, wrong row counts, invalid values, name mismatches and inconsistent overlaps. It uses `pdftotext` and does not schedule repeated downloads of the fixed historic reports. The reports are retrospective snapshots and must not be assumed available at the time of earlier forecast origins.

The separate [June 2026 press release](https://residex.nhbonline.org.in/DataUploadFormats/PressRelease_English/Press%20Release%20April-June%202026.pdf) gives year-on-year assessment-index changes for seven named cities. NHB introduced its FY 2024–25 base from the March 2026 quarter. The app shows those published percentage changes separately; they are not spliced into an older index, converted into rupees or treated as land prices. The seven-city set is a press-release selection and does not imply missing cities lack indices. No historical index is automatically joined to the experimental land model; doing that would require geography and publication-vintage review.

`.github/workflows/nhb-housing-index.yml` checks the official dashboard and at most one latest press-release PDF monthly. The collector validates the site-reported base, quarter, source host, report wording and all seven city values. It preserves previous releases, refuses a backwards quarter or invalid source, and writes only after a checked change. It does not store PDFs or download private records. A changed snapshot is tested before commit and deployment; otherwise there is no commit or deployment. Source failure fails the run and leaves the previous snapshot in place. The seven-city press-release parser is intentionally conservative: if NHB changes its format, it will need review rather than guessing. The dashboard's historical data endpoints timed out or returned an error from the development environment during initial checks; the source of the automated highlights is the public PDF, not that endpoint.
